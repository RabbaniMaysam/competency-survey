# Competency survey

A web page where students rate their own career competencies at the start and at the end of the semester, with an instructor page to open and close the two surveys, see who submitted, read each student's answers, and get a report of each round and of the change between them (page, PDF, Word, Excel).

Each class has its own survey, which the instructor edits on the Questions tab. The default survey ("Career skills self-assessment", `defaultSurvey` in `worker/src/items.js`) has 18 one-sentence statements in 6 blocks (Communication; Teamwork and leadership; Critical thinking and data; Technology; Professionalism; Career and self-development), based on the NACE career readiness competencies. Each statement is rated on a five-point confidence scale (Not at all, A little, Somewhat, Mostly, Fully), and a short introduction differs between the two rounds. An earlier survey, the NACE Career Competency Assessment Tool itself (25 items on four levels with N/A), was offered beside it until 2026-10-07 and is removed; its source PDF is not in the repository. The `survey version 2/` folder holds the LaTeX draft of a separate action-plan step that is not built.

- `docs/` holds the two pages, served by GitHub Pages: `survey.html` for students (link `survey.html?c=key`) and `survey_admin.html` for the instructor.
- `worker/` is the backend, a Cloudflare Worker with a D1 database.
- `backup/` has the daily dump of the database and its restore notes.
- `test/` holds the checks (see Tests).

No student data is stored in this repository. Each class's roster, answers, and activity log are in the database.

## How it works

A class has a roster, a title, its survey (`state.survey`), the questions removed from it (`state.retired`), and two survey rounds, "Start of semester" and "End of semester". The instructor opens a round by hand, with an optional closing time at which it closes by itself, and can close it, reopen it, or change the closing time. A student signs in, types the session code shown on the classroom screen (see below), sees the open survey (the round's introduction, then each block headed by its name and "Rate your confidence in doing the following", and each statement followed by five buttons), and answers. Answers are saved as the student goes, so the survey can be finished later. The competency cards alternate light tan and light gray, each headed by the competency's name in white on a very dark brown or near-black banner. The bar at the bottom has one circle per item, blue once answered. Submit is accepted once every item has an answer; pressing it earlier turns the unanswered circles (and items) red, and a click on a circle scrolls to its item. While the round is open, the student can change answers and submit again; the first submission time is kept. If both rounds are open at once, the student chooses which one to answer.

After submitting (and whenever the page opens on a submitted round), the student sees the results page: a card with the student's score in each section, the mean of the student's answers to its questions, as a marker on a track from 1 to 5 (the markers slide to the scores; the end-of-semester page also shows the start-of-semester score as a hollow marker). Sections answered only with the sixth option have no score. After a short pause a second card invites the student to choose up to 3 sections to improve ("Where do you want to grow?"; "What next?" on the end round), as toggle chips with the score; "Save my goals" stores them (`goals` action, `responses.goals`, logged as "choose goals"), "Not now" leaves a link to choose later. The goals can be changed while the round is open, and the chosen sections are tagged beside their scores. Under each chosen section the card shows advice for the statements the student rated lowest: every statement rated 3 or lower or answered with the sixth option, lowest first and the sixth option last, at most 3; when every statement was rated 4 or 5, the advice of the lowest-rated one, with a line saying so. Each statement is shown as a sentence on the student's answer ("You have little confidence in your ability in breaking a messy question into smaller ones you can answer."; one wording per level of the five-point scale and one for the sixth option, "You have not yet had a chance to try ..."; a scale with another number of levels gets 'You rated ... as "label".'), followed by "A good way to improve it is to " and the advice, so the advice is written to complete that sentence. The checkbox "Show the advice to students" on the Questions tab (`survey.showAdvice`, on by default, saved with the questions and copied with them) turns the advice off: students then choose goals and see none, the Worker sends neither the advice texts nor the choice, and the advice stays stored; a statement with empty advice is skipped (the Worker picks them, `adviceItems` in `worker/src/survey.js`, and sends them as `rounds[].advice`). "Review or change my answers" returns to the form, which has a "Back to my results" link.

The Questions tab edits the class's survey as a draft, saved at once after a dialog listing the changes (`saveSurvey`, logged as "save questions"): the sections (name, order, add, remove), their questions (statement and advice for the results page side by side, order, add, remove; a question or section is reordered by dragging its handle ⠿, or with the arrow keys when the handle has the focus, and a question can be dragged into another section, where its answers then count; the default advice and a new question's advice are the placeholder "Placeholder advice" until the real advice is written (the numbered placeholders of the first version, "Placeholder advice 1.1", lose their number when read); the save dialog lists the questions whose advice changed), the five option labels (the levels stay 1 to 5), the sixth option's label, the page heading, the two introductions, the instruction, the line after each section's name, and the word for a section ("Competency"/"Competencies" by default; the report uses it). A checkbox on each question adds a sixth option, "Didn't have a chance to try" by default (renamed from "No chance to try" on 2026-10-09; a class still storing the old label gets the new one when read), stored as `na`: it counts as an answer for Submit, the Responses tab and CSV show it as NA, and the report counts it in its own column and leaves it out of every mean, share, score, and change. The editor can also load the default questions or another class's. A question keeps its id when reworded, so its answers stay attached; a new question or section gets a fresh id from the Worker. The report, the Responses tab, the CSV, and the log label each question by its statement (the short labels were removed on 2026-10-09; the report's figures wrap a statement over up to three lines). A removed question moves to `retired` with its statement, and its answers stay stored (the Responses tab and CSV list it as removed; the report leaves it out); adding it back (loading the default or a copied survey) restores its answers. Removing the sixth option from a question makes its stored NA answers count as unanswered. A student who submitted answers an added question by submitting again.

A new class starts with the default survey, or (a menu next to "Create") with a copy of another class's survey and session-code settings; the roster, answers, and log are not copied.

The default competency codes end in `2` (`communication2`, `career2`, and so on) because a survey removed on 2026-10-07 used the plain codes. The report ignores any stored answer to an item not in the survey or outside the levels. A class saved before the Questions tab (2026-10-09) is converted when read (`upgrade` in `survey.js`): its survey is the default without the competencies it had turned off, whose questions are retired.

The session code: the QR code tab and the projector tab it opens (`survey_admin.html?qr=KEY`) show the student link as a QR code with a 4-digit code that changes at the class's interval (Settings: 3 to 300 seconds, 30 by default), a hash of the class's random secret and the slot number, the server clock divided by the interval (`sessionCode` in `survey.js`, copied in the instructor page, which computes it on the server's clock). A student types the code once per round; the Worker accepts the current and the previous code, then stores an empty response row, so the student can finish and change answers anywhere while the round is open. Settings turns the requirement off per class; it is on by default. The secret never leaves the Worker except to the instructor page and is left out of the JSON download.

The instructor page has eight tabs. Overview: the two rounds with their status, submission counts, open and close controls, and, for a closed round, "Delete all answers" (every student's answers to that round; the round returns to "not opened yet"). Questions: the survey editor described above. QR code: the QR code, the session code, and the live status (open round, submissions), with a button that opens the same screen in a new tab for the projector. Responses: one row per student with each round's status, each student's answers on request (with the goals the student chose), a "Delete answers" button per round (the student starts that round again), and a CSV with one row per student and round, a Goals column, and one column per item. Report: the statistics of one round ("Start of semester", "End of semester") or of the change between them ("Start vs end", for the students who submitted both), as a page with summary boxes, figures, and tables; "Download PDF" writes it as a PDF (Letter pages with page numbers, drawn with jsPDF), "Download Word" writes the same text, figures, and tables as a .docx (the figures as pictures drawn at three times their size), and "Download Excel" writes the tables plus a "Student scores" sheet with every student's competency scores in each round. A round's report has the submission counts, the mean level, the share of answers at the top two levels, a bar chart and table by competency, a 100% stacked bar chart and table of the answer levels by item, and a table of the students' scores. The "Start vs end" report has the number of students in both rounds, the mean change with the p-value of a paired t-test, the share of students whose score rose, dumbbell charts and tables (start, end, change, SD of change, students, p, rose, same, fell) by competency and by item, a chart of rose/same/fell by competency, and a table of the students' start, end, and change. Each report has a "Goals" section: a round's report counts the students who chose each competency (a bar chart and a table with the competency's mean), and the "Start vs end" report compares, per competency, the change of the students who chose it at the start with the change of those who did not; the students tables list each student's goals. A student's competency score is the mean of the student's answers to its items, and the overall score the mean of all the student's answers; competency and overall means average the students' scores, item means average the answers. The students table (names, emails, scores) is left out of the page and of the files when "students by name" is unchecked. The statistics are computed by the Worker (`worker/src/report.js`). Settings: the title, the session-code requirement and interval, a full JSON download, and deleting the class. Roster: import (Canvas gradebook export or first, last, email columns), add, remove one, or remove several at once (a checkbox per row with "Select all" and "Select none", then "Remove selected"; `removeStudents`); a removed student's answers are kept and listed as not on the roster. Log: the start of each student's round (the code typed, or the first answer when no code is required), each submission, every instructor change, and every refused attempt, with search and CSV.

Every change of consequence asks for confirmation in a dialog that states its effect: opening or closing a round, changing its closing time, deleting one student's or all students' answers, saving settings (the dialog lists what changes), importing the roster, removing one or several students, and deleting the class (two confirmations: the first lists what the class holds, from `deleteInfo`; the second needs the class key typed).

Rules are in `worker/src/survey.js`; storage is the `classes`, `responses`, and `log` tables (`worker/schema.sql`). Only the accounts in the Worker's `ADMIN_EMAILS` secret can use the instructor page.

## Roster files

The import (`parseRoster` in `worker/src/roster.js`, a copy of the attendance tool's) reads the Canvas gradebook export, with a "Student" column ("Last, First") and a "SIS Login ID" column (the address before the @, completed with `@montclair.edu`; the "Points Possible" row and Canvas's test student are skipped), or a CSV with first name, last name, and email columns in any order. Addresses are lowercased, and `@mail.montclair.edu` is stored as `@montclair.edu`; a student may sign in with either form. Importing does not replace the roster outright: a dialog (from `previewRoster`) lists the new students and the students on the roster but missing from the file, each with a checkbox, checked (kept) by default; the answers of dropped students are kept and listed as not on the roster.

## Sign-in

The pages use the "Sign in with Google" button. The Worker verifies Google's signature on the sign-in token and that the token was issued for this tool's client ID, then answers with a session token of its own (signed with the `SESSION_SECRET` secret, valid 180 days), which the page keeps in the browser's storage, so a student signs in with Google once per browser. "Sign out" deletes it. The client ID (`GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` in `worker/wrangler.toml`) is the one the group sign-up and attendance tools use; Google Cloud Console lists `https://rabbanimaysam.github.io` under its "Authorized JavaScript origins" and the student page's full address, `https://rabbanimaysam.github.io/competency-survey/survey.html`, under "Authorized redirect URIs". The redirect URI serves the "Choose or add the university account" link under the button, which opens Google's account chooser for a browser signed into a personal account. The tool holds no permission on any Google account.

## Deployment

From `worker/`, with a Cloudflare account:

```
npx wrangler d1 create competency-survey     # once; copy the database_id into wrangler.toml
npx wrangler d1 execute competency-survey --remote --file schema.sql
npx wrangler secret put ADMIN_EMAILS         # comma-separated instructor emails
npx wrangler secret put SESSION_SECRET       # any long random string (signs the session tokens)
npx wrangler deploy
```

Then write the Worker's address into `docs/config.js`. Adding an instructor is a new value of `ADMIN_EMAILS`. (On this PC use the wrangler installed at `C:\Users\rabba\tools\wrangler`, not npx.) A database created before 2026-10-09 needs the `goals` column before the Worker of that date is deployed:

```
wrangler d1 execute competency-survey --remote --command "ALTER TABLE responses ADD COLUMN goals TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'"
```

## Tests

- `node test/test_survey.mjs` checks the default survey and the rules (rounds, closing times, answers and the sixth option, settings, the upgrade of an older class, editing the questions, copying a class, roster, the student view with its section scores and goals, the goal rules, the choice of advice, the placeholder advice of a class stored before it, session codes, and that the instructor page's copy of `sessionCode` gives the same codes).
- `node test/test_report.mjs` checks the report statistics (item and competency statistics, the paired t-test and its p-value, a small class with two rounds and a section removed, the sixth option, and the goal counts and the change by goal).
- `node test/test_worker.mjs` checks a local copy of the Worker end to end (its header lists the commands; use the wrangler at `C:\Users\rabba\tools\wrangler` and a free port).
- `node test/check_pages.mjs` checks that the page scripts parse and reference no undeclared names.
